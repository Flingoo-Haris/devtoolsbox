Unix Timestamp Converter

Convert between Unix epoch timestamps and human-readable dates, both directions, in your local timezone and UTC.

Seconds & msUTC & localISO 8601

How to use the unix Timestamp Converter

  1. To convert a Unix timestamp to a date, paste it (seconds or milliseconds — detected automatically) and click Convert.
  2. To go the other way, pick a date and time in the picker and click Convert to timestamp.
  3. Results include local time, UTC, ISO 8601 format, and a relative description (e.g. “3 hours ago”).
  4. Use “Use current time” for a quick “what's the timestamp right now” lookup.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my timestamp is in seconds or milliseconds?

This tool auto-detects: Unix timestamps in seconds are 10 digits until the year 2286, while millisecond timestamps are 13 digits for the same range — the tool picks based on magnitude.

What timezone are the results in?

“Local time” uses your browser/device's configured timezone. UTC and ISO 8601 are timezone-independent (ISO 8601 is shown in UTC with a Z suffix).

Why do Java/Spring APIs sometimes expect milliseconds and sometimes seconds?

It depends on the library: java.time.Instant.getEpochSecond() gives seconds, while System.currentTimeMillis() and JavaScript's Date.now() give milliseconds — always check the field's documented unit before comparing values.

Can this handle dates before 1970?

Yes — negative Unix timestamps represent dates before January 1, 1970 UTC, and are supported.
